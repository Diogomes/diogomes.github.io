Featured project · Mobile app

Allert

Anticipating and reporting natural disasters in real time · Android (Java) with its own REST backend

Allert connects people on the street (citizens) with those who coordinate the response (Civil Defense), on top of a live map showing what is happening right now — floods, landslides, wildfires, fires and more. The user's role (defined in the JWT token) decides screens and permissions: the citizen reports incidents, follows areas and asks for help; the agency tracks the reports, edits the risk map and issues alerts to the population.

Android · Java Node.js + Express SQLite JWT + roles Real time (SSE) Google Maps Open-Meteo

Case study

1Problem

During floods, landslides, wildfires and fires, connect people on the street (citizens) with those coordinating the response (Civil Defense), with information about what is happening right now.

2Approach

Android app (Java) with its own REST backend (Node.js + Express, SQLite); the role in the JWT decides screens and permissions; alerts via SSE in a foreground service, targeted by proximity, plus a rain layer via Open-Meteo.

3Outcome

A live map: citizens report, follow areas and trigger SOS; the agency edits the risk map and issues alerts; alerts reach the phone even outside the app, only for people in the affected zone, and the community confirms reports.

Watch the video

For citizens

Detailed report (type, camera photo, GPS) or an automatic report of an emergency in a few taps. Risk map, per-area feed, tracking of your own reports and an SOS with quick dial (Civil Defense 199, Fire Department 193, SAMU 192, Police 190) and location sharing.

For the agency

List of reports (completed/not completed, with attachments) and completion marking, an editable risk map (create/edit/remove areas) and alert issuing by area, type and coverage radius for the population.

Real time (where most of the engineering went)

Alert push via SSE: a foreground service keeps a Server-Sent Events channel always open — the alert reaches the phone even outside the app, with no waiting. Proximity targeting: only people in the affected zone (radius + margin) are notified, based on real location. A near-real-time rain layer via Open-Meteo draws the coverage radius on the map colored by flood risk, and "live" reports are confirmed by the community, keeping the map updated by the crowd itself.

Case study

Build the hardest part of an MMO — not a facade prototype: a persistent world, synced in real time across multiple players and protected against cheating.
